### **🎯 Key Differences Explained**

#### **1. Execution vs Intelligence**
- **Alpaca**: "Here's the infrastructure to execute your trades." (You provide the strategy)
- **MOBU**: "Here's what to trade and why." (We provide the strategy with evidence)

**Analogy**:
- Alpaca = **Amazon Web Services** (infrastructure for developers)
- MOBU = **Bloomberg Terminal** (intelligence for investors)

#### **2. Developer vs Investor**
- **Alpaca**: Built for software engineers who can write Python/JavaScript
  - Target user: "I have a backtested algorithm, I need an API to execute it"
  - Requires technical skills (coding, algo design, backtesting)
  
- **MOBU**: Built for retail investors (African professionals, expats, advisors)
  - Target user: "I want high-conviction stock picks I can trust"
  - No coding required (point-and-click UI)

#### **3. US-Only vs Pan-African + Global**
- **Alpaca**: Only US markets (NYSE, NASDAQ, Cboe)
  - African investors **cannot trade JSE, NGX, or NSE** via Alpaca
  
- **MOBU**: African markets (JSE, NGX, NSE, EGX, GSE) + US markets (via Alpaca integration)
  - African investors can trade **local stocks** (Dangote, Safaricom, Anglo American) AND US stocks (Apple, Tesla)

#### **4. Broker vs Advisor**
- **Alpaca**: SEC-registered **broker-dealer**
  - Holds customer assets
  - Custodian of securities
  - Execution-only (no advice)
  
- **MOBU**: Registered **investment advisor** (planned)
  - Does NOT hold customer assets
  - Partners with brokers for execution
  - Provides advice/recommendations

#### **5. Payment Infrastructure**
- **Alpaca**: US bank transfers, wire transfers, ACH
  - **African investors face barriers**: Hard to fund US broker accounts from Nigeria/Kenya
  
- **MOBU**: African payment gateways (M-Pesa, Paystack, Flutterwave)
  - Seamless deposits/withdrawals in local currencies (NGN, KES, ZAR)
